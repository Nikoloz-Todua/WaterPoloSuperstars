# Milestone 30H production diagnostic

Water Polo Superstars · ToduStudios · Unity 6000.6.7f1 · Blender 5.2.3 LTS

## Verdict

The developer approved the goalkeeper improvements for now and rejected the swimwear silhouette and fit. The garment tapers sharply in the rest mesh because the reconstructed shell follows the original skin cut. Eggbeater adds substantial local distortion around the same thigh-driven openings. 30H's fabric and lighting response changes make the trim easier to see. The front panel, waist, seat and leg openings need a new fit and deformation pass.

## Production garment

- All 14 athletes, including both goalkeepers, use the enabled `CompetitionBriefs` skinned renderer with the 2,688-triangle `Assets/ProductionAthletes/Candidate/AtlasCompetitionBriefs.fbx`. The `Candidate` directory is historical. The renderer on each athlete shares the root bone and the same 66 ordered bone transforms as the cut `AthleteBody`, has 66 bind poses, and has zero enabled fallback renderers. The older 820-triangle shell supplied the pattern. The separate 2,002-triangle Meshy fitting candidate is a Blender-only historical experiment.
- In the saved Edit Mode scene, the renderers serialize the older Blue/White Competition Briefs materials with empty base maps. `AthleteEquipment.Refresh` applies the shared Blue/White Premium Kit briefs materials at runtime from the athlete's team, including the keepers. The red cap and guards are the goalkeeper distinction. The 30H kits use URP Lit, their own baked Blue/White base maps and shared fabric normal/smoothness maps. The standing comparison shows stronger band/trim separation after the bake. The prior isolated OlympicMatchArena capture shows the Blue garment through the existing water during crawl at 4.00 m/s. The underwater side angle emphasizes the thin hip section. White-specific underwater readability still needs a matched close-up. [Matched dry pair and underwater evidence](Evidence/Milestone30H/)
- The editable source is `SourceAssets/Athletes/WPS_Swimwear_001/Blender/AtlasCompetitionBriefs.blend`. It has 1,473 welded shell vertices and three open boundary loops of 109 waist, 73 and 78 leg vertices. Recovery compared the Blender mesh and confirmed that the three candidate openings coincide with the cut body's boundaries. The openings follow the 820-triangle shell within the builder's 0.3 mm registration tolerance. `Tools/Art/RebuildCompetitionBriefs.py` subdivides the old panel, retains the rim positions, and relaxes only the interior by up to 5 mm. The waist has three inset breaks around 4, 12 and 22 mm with up to 2.8 mm of raised relief, and the leg binding has inset breaks around 3.5 and 8 mm with up to 2.2 mm of relief. These are small raised strips on the old cut. They provide no independent folded band or redesigned opening curve.

### Rest shape and coverage

The front panel sweeps high over the thighs and tapers steeply toward the crotch. An older rear preview of the same geometry shows the steep leg opening around the seat as well. In Blender rest space, approximate lateral section widths for the two halves of the wrap narrow from about 415/403 mm at 60 mm below the median waist rim to 223/220 mm at 120 mm and 121/115 mm at 180 mm. This is a simple section split across the front and rear of the wrap. The 820-triangle source is within about 4 mm at the sampled levels. The triangle count went up mainly around the edges and the overall outline remained essentially the same. The hip/leg border placement and how closely the panels follow the original body surface explain the narrow, poorly wrapped look already visible in the dry standing comparison.

### Weighting and deformation

The source shell uses normalized pelvis, first spine and left/right thigh weights. Every vertex uses one to three positive influences. Blender measured 64 vertices with one, 868 with two and 541 with three, with max weight-sum error about 3.5e-8. The rim uses the corresponding skin weights. Peak source thigh influences are about 0.78 left and 0.80 right. The binding correctly keeps the garment registered to the skin. It also couples the opening closely to the driving thighs and gives the cloth no independent shape support through strong hip flexion and abduction.

The recovery sampled nine keys of the existing Blender eggbeater action. For 133 leg-rim edges at least 3 mm long at rest, the frame-by-frame 95th percentile was 2.05–2.24 times rest length, and the largest sample was about 3.07 times rest length. Some edges also compressed strongly. The same source action and the production pose captures make distortion a credible secondary cause. These ratios are local source edge stretch measurements, with no implication that the entire garment doubles in size or that Unity's runtime retarget applies exactly the same stretch.

### Body penetration and rendering limits

The recovered source audit reports zero opening separation in Blender and no additional exposed-skin polygons away from the seams in its sampled actions. The older Unity deformation log for the same FBX reports a maximum 0.098 mm seam mismatch in nine advancing eggbeater samples. Those checks establish registration. The audit found 151–175 shell/body overlap pairs in the nine Blender eggbeater samples, versus 78–94 for the old shell, within the tested seam neighborhoods. A shared boundary can contribute to an overlap count. The paired cut body has no interior skin under most of the covered area. Consequently the audit and images support local seam crowding and the risk of pinching/clipping, with penetration depth and transient near-seam crossings still unresolved. They provide no evidence of broad skin breakthrough as the cause of the triangular front. The earlier ray-coverage test used the old garment as its target, so high measured coverage meant that the new shell followed the old pattern.

The 30H bake only changes the fabric response and cannot move the waist or leg curves. The current dry silhouette, matched binding and underwater visibility account for the rejected shape without a missing renderer, wrong skin binding or underwater shader failure. Light, water angle and fabric contrast can still change how strongly the edges read. No isolated material or binding correction would address the confirmed rest cut and pose distortion.

## Goalkeeper diagnosis and correction

Before 30H, a general Block flag and a forward-reaching rest target kept the arms high and divorced the contact sphere at the lifted carry anchor from the rendered hand. The production rig now keeps the existing aquatic base pose, layers low neutral arms and ball tracking, reads goal-local approach and time to contact, and drives bounded preparation, body rise, water press and one- or two-hand reach. GoalkeeperInterception sweeps the evaluated palms before PrototypeMatch checks scoring. BallController applies all catches and deflections, using the established right-hand grip for possession. Unrigged configurations retain the prior compatibility path.

The initial fixture showed a slow reaction to the fast near-post delivery and an arm blend frozen during goal presentation. Before interruption the previous session shortened reaction for high-speed threats and allowed a settling keeper to finish recovery even during the goal presentation. The focused recovery confirmed a right-side parry, central high parry, and a legitimate scored near-post miss, with save blend returning to zero. The [final report](MILESTONE_30H_FINAL_REPORT.md) records the exact results and prior catch evidence.

## Next authorized fitting pass

1. Duplicate the editable garment into an isolated Blender candidate. Recover the uncut Atlas skin or an equivalent complete fitting reference on the same 66-bone rig. Use actual front, rear and side competition-brief references. Set waist rise, side depth, front coverage, seat wrap and smoothly curved leg openings in rest pose before material work. Give the waistband and bindings independent edge loops and practical garment thickness. Compare matched orthographic silhouettes against the current shell.
2. Rebuild the skin cut from the approved candidate openings. Inspect rest clearance and triangle crossings against the full fitting reference before cutting. Register the exposed skin to the shell at all three seams, with shared weights where the seam meets. Tune pelvis-to-thigh gradients inward from each rim. Keep waistband support mainly on pelvis/lower spine, preserve leg opening shape through upper-thigh movement and evaluate bounded pose corrections only where weighting alone leaves significant pinching. Preserve gameplay clips and their approved motion.
3. Profile matched front, back and sides in neutral, several eggbeater phases including maximal knee separation, keeper high reach, normal/sprint crawl, holds, passes and throws. Compare per-region edge strain, seam distance, and actual penetration depth/contact away from intentionally shared borders. Track front/rear coverage and repeat the test on the uncut fitting reference and the dressed cut body. The existing walking clip may serve as a fit pose without any walking implementation work. Keep rendering and bone influence costs comparable to the current garment.
4. Import one isolated candidate in the existing Test Lab and compare dry and submerged Blue and White Game views. Confirm material overrides, seams and readability through the real water, including close-up and match framing. Obtain the developer's visual approval before any roster-wide replacement. Preserve fallback, production arena GUID and real profile throughout the work.
