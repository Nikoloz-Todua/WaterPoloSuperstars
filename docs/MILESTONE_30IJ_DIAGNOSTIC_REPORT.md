# Milestones 30I and 30J diagnostic report

## Workstream B · Movement and physical continuity

- The 20 × 25 m rule rectangle previously doubled as the swimmer clamp. The 1.10 m field-athlete margin and keeper station clamp were independent of the visible 22.5 × 28.6 m basin. The goal pocket existed only for the ball. The roster’s kinematic contact solver reused the same restrictive swimmer clamp for separation corrections.
- SwimmerMotor and its contact solver now share one physical basin clamp with the 0.40 m authored capsule radius. The independent arena dimensions asset supplies the outside walls. The three goal obstacles use the existing post, side-net and rear-net dimensions. The 3 m opening and 1 m depth permit a centred root about 0.50 m inside after a 0.10 m rear stroke allowance was added during visual review. The transverse and depth constraints preserve tangential input. The competition buoys have no authored solid collider and are treated as traversable surface markers for swimmers and rule boundaries for the ball.
- The keeper tracked field-side attacks and had no loose-ball branch. Its automatic station clamp also suppressed backward motion. Global pickup accepted only ball centres inset from the rule rectangle. A near-goal loose-ball branch now releases the station clamp, turns into the ball, and moves through SwimmerMotor. BallController retains eligibility, velocity, reach and facing checks. A fully crossed scoring ball is denied pickup before the match scores it. The fast 30H save path, timings and shot tuning are retained.
- MatchRules immediately called RetireForReplacement, which stopped physics, disabled the collider, and hid the real ball. The existing setup delay now uses a visible dynamic continuation. BallInMotion and acquisition/score flags close immediately, last touch is retained and the same body preserves momentum. Airborne movement, the water entry response and a shallow, bounded deck response run until replacement. Setup is about 0.85 s for airborne exits and 0.60 s for surface exits at default values.
- The full-ball goal crossing remains at the front of the posts. The painted side boundary and the end rope 0.30 m beyond the scoring plane use swept whole-sphere exits. A defending keeper touch, a defending field player's blocked shot, or a qualified deliberate defending touch can award a corner. Other untouched attacker misses award a goal throw. A blocked shot crossing a sideline retains the existing field-block exception. These use last-touch facts captured before any dead-ball contacts. [Current World Aquatics Part Six, articles 7 and 12–14](https://resources.fina.org/fina/document/2026/11/26/a63658dd-6078-4ef8-ab2a-2da081347f6b/_2026-11-26_World-Aquatics_CR.pdf)
- The project retains its simplified restart marks and limited direct-shot permissions, its existing clock settings, and its existing goal-area and foul coverage. It has no general contact-adjudication rule for a swimmer voluntarily leaving the marked rectangle, no holding-above-rope adjudication, and the decorative buoys have no physical rebound. Those discrepancies are separate rules or equipment work.

## Workstream A · Natural match continuity

- Ordinary fouls previously used StopPlay, which locked selection, called StopMotion, disabled all roster motors, and held setup for the generic 0.8 s. ArrangeRestart also repositioned participants by resetting poses. Tactics later returned with a basic free-throw target. Ordinary awards now hold the taker only, retain athlete velocities through selection changes, and mark the throw ready once the ball is awarded. Attackers use the 30F shared pocket sampler and defenders use their existing assignments under the 1 m ordinary defensive stand-off. Match rules still own the allowed release and keep clocks stopped until legal release. Penalty, exclusion, period, sprint and centre phases keep their separate policy.
- Goals previously used the same global roster stop. The presentation also hid the actual athletes and redrew a single recorded frame with one arm pose. Scoring still happens once in PrototypeMatch. The short celebration now uses the moving live rigs and bounded post-goal swimming. The director assigns a gesture using score margin, late-match impact, nearby teammates, recent pass source and the previous choice. The same gesture does not play on the entire roster. Replay uses the existing two recorded views and no second scoring event. The centre restart keeps fields already on the legal side, stages those still out of position and awards the conceding side.
- The movement and reaction approach takes inspiration from the public descriptions of humanization and varied goal moments. The gestures use the project's own evaluated arms, head, torso and eggbeater support. [EA Sports gameplay notes](https://www.ea.com/ea-play/news/pitch-notes-fifa-22-gameplay-deep-dive)

## Local water effects

The old idle ripple alternated hands on a timer and derived wash from hand position relative to the root, regardless of hand motion. The advancing-pose sampler now measures planar hand velocity near the surface and staggers short per-hand cooldowns. Crawl still uses armed water-height crossings. Submerged pulls, sprint wakes, ball-leading bow disturbances, subtle floating rings, and strength scaled to observed impact reuse the existing mesh, history and quality caps. The restart and live celebration phases can sample moving skin. Approved skeletal motion and skip eligibility are retained.
