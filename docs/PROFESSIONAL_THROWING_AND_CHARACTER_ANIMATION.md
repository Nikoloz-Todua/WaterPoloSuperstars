# Milestone 30 — Professional throwing and athlete presentation

## Why Milestone 29 carry could lag

`BallController` moved the held ball early in LateUpdate (execution order −140) toward an exponentially smoothed swimmer-root offset. The same nominal point drove Humanoid wrist IK during Animator evaluation. The right wrist could only reach the clamped IK target, and `RiggedSwimmerVisual` then moved the visual hierarchy to the waterline at order 180. The apparent lag persisted with held Rigidbody interpolation already disabled. A separate problem affected quick throws. The previous launch method spawned the ball at a synthetic socket, then raised `Released`, which started the throwing animation.

## Evaluated grip and release

- `BallController` remains the only physical ball and ownership authority. The production switch **Professional Throws** is serialized on its arena instance. A compatible, active Humanoid supplies the grip after standard Animator evaluation, waterline alignment and final visual solving. Kinematic Rigidbody and render position read the same point. `GoalReplayDirector` captures the final skeleton and ball at order 310 through the current recorded-transform system.
- `WaterPoloThrowPose` builds a right-hand frame from the bind wrist and index/little knuckle bases. The palm location includes a skin offset and the actual physical ball radius along its palm normal. Shoulder and elbow lengths come from the current rig. The elbow pole and wrist bend limit guide a two-bone solve. The right-hand correction is applied once. The free arm and lower body stay on the existing tread animation. The existing Atlas hand surface is rigid-weighted, so these changes provide palm orientation without individual finger deformation.
- The low hold is forward and outboard of the shoulder, with the palm supporting the ball. Holding shot charge moves gradually toward a high rear load. The loaded elbow bends while the chest turns away from the intended direction. Acceleration brings the shoulder forward and extends the elbow. The follow-through blends back over 0.22 seconds. Gaze follows the requested shot direction or the selected teammate within a limited yaw and pitch.
- Pickup and dry catch use the existing physical eligibility and reach. Newly collected balls and decelerating dribblers travel from the previous ball point toward the palm over up to 0.13 seconds. The receiving arm reaches toward that starting point. The existing lead and roll calculations remain available during controlled surface swimming. With the switch on, the lead is evaluated after the latest head pose. Logical possession does not change during the gather.

## Action timing

| Live action | Preparation before release | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Stationary standard shot | 0.23 s | Rear load, overhead acceleration, full follow-through |
| Quick shot while dribbling or gathering | 0.14 s | Compact load after any outstanding gather |
| Short pass or pass from dribble/gather | 0.12 s | Compact load, lower release height target |
| Longer pass | 0.18 s | More loading and shoulder rotation than the short pass |
| Lob | 0.20 s | Higher release posture |

Durations use scaled game time and release on the first evaluated pose reaching the marker. A request returns success when accepted. Ownership and launch change when the marker is evaluated, and a second request cannot queue during that interval. The pending action holds the aiming direction, chosen receiver, speed, shot type and receiving parameters. The current ballistic formulas, score attribution, release protection, receiving gates and existing events are shared with the legacy path. BallController checks owner, phase, eligibility and receiver at the marker, clears the request before committing, and cancels it on pause, match suspension, new ownership or ball reset. The delayed pose currently covers live play. Protected penalty, boundary and centre restarts still use their previous release behavior and configured marks.

Field AI and goalkeeper distribution wait for a pending turn of the throwing arm before choosing again. Their counters and tactical pass feedback use the physical `Released` event. A human cannot start a second charge during the pending interval. A pass from an evaluated palm starts at the palm height and targets the established receiving-hand height. The existing catch sphere and eligibility checks still resolve contact.

The overhead motion is based on the observed bent-elbow backswing and late elbow extension in filmed water polo throws. ## Aquatic presentation

Under the same switch, the authored crawl head-joint target is 0.087 m above the waterline, a 0.033 m reduction from Milestone 29. At low locomotion the eggbeater target remains 0.15 m. The visual driver blends a modest forward/down gaze while swimming and tracks the ball on keepers during live play, with limited head rotation. Keeper interception, probabilities, save clips and block selection remain under the existing system. The existing editable crawl, sprint and eggbeater Actions and their wrist/foot curves remain the source clips. The throw solver changes elbow bend and wrist direction only during the staged possession motion. Per-direction keeper arm choreography and new aquatic wrist animation Actions are outside this delivered change.

The ceremony's deck-correction option retains the imported Humanoid walk and the 1.36 m stride calibration. `DeckFootPlacement` identifies stance from the lower heel and toe, keeps the low foot close to its last contact while the route advances, then fades the correction as the foot lifts or reaches its allowed travel. A two-bone adjustment preserves knee bending, and the existing ceremony timeline still owns the route and stage progression.

## Inspection and recovery

In saved Edit Mode use **Tools > Water Polo > Arena > Apply Professional Throwing** to enable the feature and **Restore Previous Throwing** under the same menu to recover the prior hold, throw timing and waterline posture. The production arena and its original animation assets stay available. During Play Mode, selecting a rig in the Scene view shows its grip, wrist connection and local waterline as gizmos. Use **Tools > Water Polo > Play Olympic Match Arena** to inspect the live paths, then restore **Tools > Water Polo > Recovery > Use Boot as Editor Play Entry**. With a stationary selected carrier, turn in place and hold Space to inspect the loaded pose. Tap Space or B from a dribble for the compact motion.

`Tools/Verification/ProfessionalThrowVerification.cs` contains the isolated advancing Play Mode fixture. It enables the profile temporarily, skips ceremony, parks other athletes and records per-frame data and screenshots under `Logs/Milestone30/Throwing/`. It samples the gameplay camera at the end of rendering and listens for the real physical release event. The test covers full-circle and alternating turns, palm alignment, measured release position, duplicate suppression, pause and reset cancellation, staged shots and three physical pass catches. The Windows Editor gate passed 132 rendered turn samples with 0.000 m separation between the evaluated grip and ball. Minimum measured distance from ball center to the head joint was 0.558 m. In the charged pose the ball was 0.390 m behind the head joint, measured along the facing direction. The standard shot and dribbling quick shot took 0.232 s and 0.273 s from request to release. The short, long and dribbling quick passes took 0.149 s, 0.207 s and 0.253 s and were caught by the staged teammate. All five physical launches matched the evaluated grip in the release callback, duplicate commands were rejected, and pause and ball reset cancelled pending throws.

The initial probe sampled a render callback that preceded the final evaluated gameplay-camera pose and produced a false discrepancy. The successful gate measured after the exact gameplay camera rendered. The first short pass also exposed a receiving-height regression. The final gate used the established 0.57 m receiving target above the waterline and the original catch radius. Raw measurements are in `Logs/Milestone30/ThrowingGate.json`, `Logs/Milestone30/Throwing/Turn-Trace.txt` and `Logs/Milestone30/Throwing/Release-Trace.txt`. The fixture drove motor and gameplay APIs with parked opposition. It measured ball-to-joint clearance, and phone input, every contact pose, and a competitive full-length match were outside this focused gate.
